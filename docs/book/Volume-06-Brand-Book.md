## 1. Brand Identity Philosophy

### 1.1 What Certilab Stands For

Certilab is a professional platform for Technical Architects (Arquitectos Técnicos). The brand exists to represent competence, trust, and independence in the energy certification audit process.

The brand archetype is the **Expert / Judge** — not the Sage (who seeks truth for its own sake), but the Judge who applies expertise to make determinations that affect real outcomes.

**Brand essence:** Rigour. Independence. Precision.

**Brand promise:** The professional's tool for technical truth.

### 1.2 Brand Values

| Value | Meaning | Manifestation |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Rigour** | Every audit follows a structured, repeatable methodology | The PITR™ system, the question tree, the quality review process |
| **Independence** | Unbiased technical assessment, free from commercial pressure | Second opinion model, transparency in methodology |
| **Precision** | Exact language, exact measurements, exact conclusions | Technical accuracy in reports, clear communication |
| **Professionalism** | Built by professionals, for professionals | Interface language, workflow design, domain expertise |
| **Trust** | Both the AT and the client must trust the platform | Transparency, consistency, reliability |

### 1.3 Brand Personality

If Certilab were a person, it would be:

- A **senior architectural technician** with 20+ years of experience.
- **Confident** but not arrogant.
- **Quiet** — speaks when there is something important to say.
- **Methodical** — follows a process, does not cut corners.
- **Trustworthy** — their word is their bond.
- **Independent** — not tied to any construction company, promoter, or manufacturer.

### 1.4 The Brand Metaphor

> **Certilab is to energy certification what a notary is to a contract.**
> 
> Independent verification. Expert assessment. Binding in its accuracy.

This metaphor guides all brand decisions:
- The brand presents itself with the gravitas of a professional institution.
- The visual identity should suggest certification, verification, stamps, seals.
- The tone is one of quiet authority, not marketing enthusiasm.

## 2. Brand Visual Identity

### 2.1 Logo Usage Guidelines

The Certilab logo consists of two elements: the wordmark and the icon (the "C" mark — pending validation of design).

#### Clear Space

The minimum clear space around the logo is equal to the height of the letter "C" in the wordmark. No other elements should intrude into this space.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                              │
│     ← [clear space] → [LOGO] ← [clear →    │
│                                              │
│     Clear space = height of "C"              │
│                                              │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Minimum Size

| Context | Minimum Width | Format |
|---------|--------------|--------|
| Digital (screen) | 120px | PNG/SVG |
| Print | 25mm | Vector |
| Favicon | 32×32px | ICO/SVG |
| Social media avatar | 400×400px | PNG |

#### Incorrect Logo Uses

**Never:**
- Stretch or distort the logo.
- Change the colours outside the approved palette.
- Add shadows, gradients, or effects.
- Rotate the logo.
- Place the logo on low-contrast backgrounds.
- Rearrange the wordmark and icon.
- Add taglines or other text to the logo.
- Use the logo as a pattern or repeating element.

**✅ Correct:**
- Logo on white background (primary).
- Logo on brand blue background (reversed/white version).
- Logo at minimum size with adequate clear space.

**❌ Incorrect:**
- Logo stretched horizontally.
- Logo on a photographic background with no contrast.
- Logo with drop shadow.
- Logo used as a watermark over text.

### 2.2 Logo Variations

| Variation | Usage | Description |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| Full logo (primary) | Most applications | Wordmark + icon, horizontal |
| Icon only | Favicon, app icon, avatar | "C" mark alone |
| Horizontal | Website header, documents | Standard layout |
| Vertical (stacked) | Narrow spaces, mobile | Icon above wordmark |
| Reversed/white | Dark backgrounds | White version of logo |
| Monochrome | Single-colour applications | Black or brand blue only |

### 2.4 Typography

The typography system is defined in Volume II (Visual Language). Brand-specific usage:

| Context | Typeface | Weight | Size |
|---------|----------|--------|------|
| Logo text | System sans-serif or custom logo font | Bold | Custom |
| Headings | Inter | Semibold (600) | 24-48px |
| Body text | Inter | Regular (400) | 14-16px |
| UI labels | Inter | Medium (500) | 12-14px |
| Code/data | JetBrains Mono | Regular (400) | 12-14px |

**Typography rules (brand-specific):**
- No decorative or display fonts in V1.
- No more than two typefaces in any application.
- Line height: 1.5 for body text, 1.2 for headings.

### 2.5 Photography Style

> **Pending validation of design.** The following principles are provisional.

**Principles for V1:**
- No stock photography of people using computers.
- Use architectural and building photography that is technically precise.
- Images should convey: precision, profession, materiality.
- Black and white or desaturated colour palette for photography.
- Focus on details (plans, tools, materials) rather than people.

**Style reference:**
- Architectural photography (Josef Schulz, Hélène Binet).
- Detail shots of construction elements.
- Plans and technical drawings.
- Material textures (concrete, wood, metal, glass).

**For V1:**
- Use high-quality architectural photography from stock libraries.
- Convert to black and white or a single-tone palette.
- Apply subtle dark overlay for text readability.

### 2.6 Illustration Style

> **Pending validation of design.** The following principles are provisional.

**Principles for V2+:**
- Technical illustration style (similar to architectural diagrams).
- Isometric or orthogonal projection.
- Minimal detail, clear lines.
- Single colour or limited palette.
- No character illustrations.
- Purpose: explain concepts, not decorate.

**For V1:**
- No illustrations. Use iconography only (see Volume III: Design System).

## 3. Brand Voice

### 3.1 Voice Principles

The brand voice is defined in detail in Volume V (Copywriting). This section summarises the principles as they relate to brand identity.

| Principle | Explanation |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Competent** | Speaks with authority. Knows the domain. Uses correct terminology. |
| **Clear** | Prefers simple, direct language. Never uses jargon for its own sake. |
| **Calm** | No shouting. No exclamation marks. No hype. No urgency. |
| **Respectful** | Trusts the user's intelligence. Does not over-explain. Does not patronise. |
| **Brief** | Says what needs to be said and stops. |

### 3.2 What the Brand Never Says

- "Innovación disruptiva"
- "Soluciones integrales"
- "Empoderando profesionales"
- "Ecosistema digital"
- "Líderes en el sector"
- "Transformación digital"
- "Sinergias"
- "Value-added services"
- "Revolucionario"
- "Único en el mercado"

### 3.3 Voice in Different Contexts

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| AT-facing UI | Direct, professional, technical | "Revisa los documentos antes de iniciar el análisis." |
| Client-facing UI | Warm, clear, accessible | "Tu solicitud ha sido recibida. Te notificaremos cuando un AT comience la revisión." |
| Marketing | Confident, authoritative | "La segunda opinión técnica para certificados energéticos." |
| Support | Helpful, patient, precise | "Para subir un documento, haz clic en 'Subir archivo' y selecciona el PDF." |
| Social media | Professional, informative | "¿Sabías que el 30% de los certificados energéticos contienen errores? La segunda opinión es clave." |

## 4. Brand Applications

### 4.1 Website / Landing Page

The website is the primary public face of the brand. It must convey trust and professionalism from the first interaction.

**Layout principles:**
- Clean, spacious layout with generous white space.
- Hero section with clear value proposition.
- Three-step explanation of the service.
- Trust signals section (credentials, testimonials, numbers).
- Professional typography and colour palette.
- No animations or decorative elements that distract from the message.

**Page structure:**
```
1. Hero (value proposition + CTA)
2. How it works (3 steps)
3. Why Certilab (differentiators)
4. Trust signals (AT credentials, case numbers)
5. Testimonials (client and AT quotes)
6. CTA section (final conversion)
7. Footer (legal, links, contact)
```

### 4.2 Email Communications

**Transactional emails (auth, notifications):**
- Minimal design, single column.
- Brand colour for headers and links.
- Clean typography, generous spacing.
- No images except the logo.
- Focus on the message, not the decoration.

**Marketing emails:**
- Same structure as transactional, plus optional hero image.
- Subject line: direct, informative, no clickbait.
- Preheader text: concise summary.
- CTA as a button (brand blue, white text).

**Email templates:**
| Template | Purpose | Frequency |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Confirmation | "Tu solicitud ha sido recibida" | Transactional |
| Notification | "Un AT ha comenzado la revisión" | Transactional |
| Corrections | "Necesitamos documentación adicional" | Transactional |
| Delivery | "Tu segunda opinión está lista" | Transactional |
| Follow-up | "¿Cómo fue tu experiencia?" | 7 days post-delivery |
| Newsletter | Industry updates, tips | Monthly (V2+) |

### 4.3 Social Media

> **Pending validation of design.** Social media strategy will be developed as part of the go-to-market plan.

**Principles:**
- LinkedIn is the primary platform (professional audience).
- Content: industry analysis, technical articles, regulatory updates.
- No promotional posts. Focus on value and expertise.
- Visuals: architectural photography, data visualisations, report excerpts.
- Tone: professional, informative, never salesy.

**Platform priorities:**
1. LinkedIn (primary)
2. Twitter/X (secondary, for industry news)
3. Instagram (tertiary, for brand awareness, V2+)

### 4.4 Presentations and Decks

**Presentation template:**
- Title slide: Logo + title. White background. Minimal.
- Content slides: Title top-left. Content in main area. Page number bottom-right.
- Typography: Inter, same sizing as brand guidelines.
- Colours: Brand blue for headers, neutral for body text.
- No transitions or animations.
- Data slides: Simple charts, clear labels, source citations.

**Deck structure:**
1. Cover (logo + title)
2. Problem statement (1 slide)
3. Solution (1-2 slides)
4. How it works (1-2 slides)
5. Market opportunity (1-2 slides)
6. Business model (1 slide)
7. Team (1 slide)
8. Contact (1 slide)

### 4.5 Print Materials

> **Future (V2+).** Principles only.

- Business cards: Logo + name + contact. Minimal.
- Brochures: Single A4 tri-fold. Clean layout.
- Reports: Professional binding. Brand colours. Technical.
- Letterhead: Logo + contact. Standard format.

## 5. Brand in Context

### 5.1 Certilab on the AT's Screen

The AT interface is the most important brand touchpoint for the primary user.

**Brand expressions in the AT interface:**
- Logo in the top-left navigation.
- Brand blue as the primary action colour.
- Neutral, clean backgrounds.
- Professional, direct language throughout.
- Consistent spacing and typography.
- No promotional content or upsells.

> **The interface is the brand.** Every interaction is a brand experience.

### 5.2 Certilab on the Client's Phone

The client interface is the second most important brand touchpoint.

**Brand expressions in the client interface:**
- Same visual language as the AT interface, with simpler content.
- Mobile-optimised with large touch targets.
- Trust signals visible throughout (credentials, security).
- Professional language, accessible vocabulary.
- Clear status communication.

### 5.3 Certilab in Email

Email is the primary communication channel for clients.

**Brand expressions in email:**
- Logo in the header.
- Brand blue for links and CTAs.
- Professional closing signature.
- Consistent sender name (Certilab Platform).
- No marketing tone in transactional emails.

### 5.4 Certilab in Social Proof / Testimonials

Testimonials and case studies must feel authentic, not staged.

**Guidelines:**
- Use real names and credentials (with permission).
- Use full company/role information.
- Quote the specific benefit, not a generic statement.
- Format as a quote with attribution.
- Include context (e.g., "AT con 15 años de experiencia en Barcelona").

**✅ Correct:**
> "Certilab me ha permitido ofrecer un servicio más completo a mis clientes. El proceso es claro y el PITR™ asegura que no se me pasa nada."
> — Jordi Martínez, Arquitecto Técnico, Barcelona

**❌ Incorrect:**
> "Certilab es la mejor plataforma del mercado. La recomiendo a todos mis colegas."
> — Un cliente satisfecho

## 6. Brand Guidelines

### 6.1 What to Always Do

- Use the full logo on the website and primary documents.
- Maintain the brand colour palette.
- Use professional, calm language.
- Keep layouts clean with generous white space.
- Prioritise readability and accessibility.
- Use Spanish as the primary language.
- Use technical vocabulary correctly.

### 6.2 What to Never Do

- **Never use the logo on low-contrast backgrounds.**
- **Never add decorative elements that don't serve a purpose.**
- **Never use corporate jargon or empty marketing language.**
- **Never use stock photography of people in business suits.**
- **Never use exclamation marks in UI text.**
- **Never add confetti, celebrations, or gamification elements.**
- **Never use the brand for political or religious statements.**
- **Never alter the logo proportions or colours.**

### 6.3 Partner/Co-Branding Guidelines

> **Pending validation of design.** The following principles are provisional.

- Co-branded materials must maintain Certilab's visual identity.
- Partner logo must be smaller than or equal to Certilab logo.
- Both logos must maintain their clear space.
- The partnership must be clearly described (e.g., "en colaboración con").
- Certilab's brand guidelines take precedence in all materials.

## 7. Brand Evolution Plan

### 7.1 V1 Brand (Current)

The V1 brand is minimal and functional. The priority is clarity, not brand embellishment.

**V1 brand scope:**
- Logo: Full logo + icon. No tagline.
- Colours: Primary palette + neutrals. No secondary palette.
- Typography: Inter system font.
- Photography: Architectural stock photography.
- Illustrations: None. Iconography only.
- Applications: Web app, marketing website, email.

### 7.2 V2 Brand Evolution

**V2 additions (post-MVP, subject to roadmap):**
- Full illustration system (technical illustration style).
- Extended colour palette for data visualisation.
- Photography guidelines (custom photography).
- Illustration guidelines (custom illustrations).
- Brand applications expansion (social media, print, video).
- Comprehensive partner/co-branding guidelines.

### 7.3 Brand Maturity (V3+)

**V3+ considerations:**
- Potential for sub-brands (Certilab for ATs, Certilab for clients).
- Brand architecture for international markets if applicable.
- Motion design guidelines.
- Voice and tone expansion for new channels.

## 8. Open Brand Questions

| ID | Question | Context | Depends On |
|----|----------|---------|------------|
| BR-001 | Should the logo include a tagline (e.g., "Peritaje Técnico")? | A tagline provides context but adds complexity. Most professional brands (Apple, Stripe) use no tagline. | Brand strategy validation |
| BR-002 | What is the official logo icon? | The "C" mark is proposed but not yet finalised. Should it be purely typographic or include a symbol? | Design validation |
| BR-003 | Should the brand use a secondary colour palette? | Currently limited to primary + neutral. A secondary palette could add flexibility for V2. | V2 planning |
| BR-004 | What is the brand's position on gender representation in communications? | The AT profession is male-dominated. Should brand imagery reflect this reality or aim for aspirational representation? | Brand strategy validation |
| BR-005 | Should the brand name include "Platform" in all references? | "Certilab Platform" is the full name. "Certilab" is used informally. When should each be used? | Brand strategy validation |
| BR-006 | How should the brand handle crisis communications? | A brand built on trust must have protocols for service outages, data incidents, or inaccurate reports. | V2 planning |
| BR-007 | Should the brand have a physical presence (office, events)? | A physical presence could reinforce the professional positioning, especially for client trust. | V2 planning |
